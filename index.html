One country, fifty stories

Every state page on this site covers the same ground a curious traveler or student actually needs: the capital and major cities, state symbols and how they were chosen, the landmarks worth the drive, the foods the state claims as its own, and the moments when that state changed American history. Alabama's civil rights landmarks, Arizona's desert parks, Georgia's capital in Atlanta, Wyoming's first national park — the country is too varied for one story, so we tell fifty of them, plus the District of Columbia and the territories.

For families, the Kids Zone turns that same material into play: capital sprint games, flag matching, landmark dashes, and state scrambles that teach geography the way kids actually learn it — by doing it, repeatedly, for fun. Teachers and homeschool parents use the state pages as reading material and the games as review. There is no account, no paywall, and nothing to download.

Good news, on purpose

The Updates feed leads with what is working: parks reopening, landmarks restored, communities rebuilding, records set, and ordinary people doing extraordinary things in all fifty states. It sits alongside the practical guides and the history, because a site about the United States should leave you knowing more — and feeling better — than when you arrived. New stories are added regularly; the state guides are updated as facts change. Welcome in.
